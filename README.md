## Master's Research Dissertation

### Factors Influencing the Adoption and Effectiveness of Drone-Based Medical Transport: A Systematic Review

* **Author:** Charitha Balaka Venkatareddy
* **Supervisor:** Dr Martyn Bradley
* **Awarding Department:** University of Liverpool Management School
* **Date of Submission:** January 2026
* **Full Text:** [`coursework/Medical-Drone-Transport-SLR.pdf`](coursework/Medical-Drone-Transport-SLR.pdf)

#### Research Scope & Methodological Design
The dissertation investigates why routine operational integration of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in healthcare logistics remains limited despite widespread feasibility demonstrations. Adopting a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) methodology following **PRISMA 2020** standards, 186 initial records from five scholarly databases (Business Source Complete, Scopus, Emerald Insight, Web of Science, PubMed) were screened, resulting in a synthesized core dataset of **37 empirical studies and 9 foundational theoretical works**. Methodological quality was evaluated using the **Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT 2018)**.

#### Conceptual Contributions & Synthesis Themes
1. **The Sociotechnical Divergence (Strategic Intent vs. Organizational Readiness):**  
   Applying the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), the research identifies a structural disconnect between executive enthusiasm during subsidized pilots and front-line operational readiness. Institutional barriers—including regulatory restrictions on Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS), hospital liability, and workflow disruption—frequently prevent permanent adoption once trial funding ends.
2. **The Validity Gradient (Payload Complexity & Clinical Safety):**  
   Evidence across biological transport indicates a gradient of empirical certainty: while standard blood products (RBCs) show high biochemical stability during flight, evidence for fragile biologicals (monoclonal antibodies, organs) is preliminary and documents significant risks of shear-induced protein aggregation and pressure-related degradation.
3. **Simulation Optimism vs. Ecological Reality:**  
   Mathematical routing and dispatch models systematically overestimate drone efficiency by assuming deterministic weather, unrestricted airspace, and zero dispatch friction, contrasting with empirical reports of weather-induced cancellations and urban signal attenuation.

## Theoretical & Methodological Foundations

The research and coursework across this portfolio draw upon established management theories:

- **Technology Adoption & Organizational Change:** Technology Acceptance Model (Davis, 1989), Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (Venkatesh et al., 2003), Diffusion of Innovations (Rogers, 2003), Kotter's Change Model.
- **Strategic Management & Competitive Advantage:** Resource-Based View (Barney, 1991), Five Forces & Generic Strategies (Porter, 1985), Blue Ocean Strategy (Kim & Mauborgne, 2005), Product-Market Matrix (Ansoff, 1957).
- **Organizational Behaviour & Group Decision-Making:** Team Roles (Belbin, 1981), Stages of Group Development (Tuckman, 1965), Work Team Design (Hackman, 1987), Bounded Rationality (Simon, 1955), Principled Negotiation (Fisher & Ury, 1991), Experiential Learning (Kolb, 1984).
- **Research Methodologies:** PRISMA 2020 systematic review protocol, Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (Hong et al., 2018), Driscoll's reflective framework.
